01 — Overview

One app for the entire party

The client — an opposition political party — needed a mobile platform to coordinate members, manage events, onboard new activists, and maintain internal communications. The key requirement: everything had to live in one app, with sensitive leadership data protected by role-based access.

Role 01

Leadership

Full access to all modules, internal analytics, sensitive communications, and member management.

Role 02

Members

Established party members. Access to events, messenger, news feed, and AI agent.

Standard access
Role 03

New Activists

Recently joined via referral. Limited visibility — cannot access sensitive leadership content until trusted.

Limited access
02 — Problem

Scattered tools, no accountability

Before this app, the party had no unified digital infrastructure. Events were announced through scattered channels. New activists joined with no clear onboarding path, and there was no way to track who brought whom into the organization.

No reliable event notifications

Members missed events because announcements were spread across Telegram, email, and word of mouth with no central source of truth.

No referral accountability

New activists joined with no traceable path — it was impossible to know who recruited whom or reward active members for growing the network.

No access control

Internal communications had no middle ground — anyone could see everything, or nothing at all.

Key constraint: The access control system had to be invisible to end users — new activists should not feel excluded, but sensitive leadership content simply shouldn't appear in their interface.

03 — Research

Understanding three different users

The challenge with this project was that there wasn't one user — there were three, with completely different needs, mental models, and levels of trust. I mapped each user type's core jobs-to-be-done and identified where their journeys overlapped and where they diverged.

This directly shaped the information architecture: a shared shell with role-filtered content, rather than separate apps or obvious "blocked" screens.

Key design insight

Access control should be architectural, not visual. New members shouldn't encounter locked doors — they simply shouldn't see doors that don't belong to them yet.

05 — Design

Four modules, one coherent system

Core

Event notifications

A centralized events feed with push notifications replaced fragmented Telegram announcements. Members can RSVP, see location, and add events to their calendar.

Unique

QR referral system

Every member gets a personal QR code. When scanned during onboarding, the system automatically links the new activist to their recruiter — creating a traceable, accountable growth network.

Core

Internal messenger

Role-filtered group chats and direct messages. Leadership channels are invisible to new activists — not locked, simply absent from their navigation.

Unique

AI agent

A chat interface trained on the party's official positions and policy documents. Members get instant, accurate answers without waiting for a human response.

07 — Reflection

What I'd do differently

The most complex part of this project wasn't the visual design — it was designing a system that works coherently across three fundamentally different user types. In hindsight, I would have pushed for usability testing with actual activists before finalizing the onboarding flow, particularly the QR code experience.

The AI agent module was also a first for me. Given more time, I would have explored conversation design patterns specific to civic and policy contexts.

"Designing for a politically sensitive context adds a layer of responsibility that sharpens every decision — not just about usability, but about trust, transparency, and what information people deserve to see."
